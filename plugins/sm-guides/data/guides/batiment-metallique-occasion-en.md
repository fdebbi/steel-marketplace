# Buying or selling a used steel building

A used steel building is sold in two ways on ReuSteel: **assembled**, still standing on its site, or **dismantled**, as elements ready to collect. Either way, the buyer must check the dimensions, the condition and what the price includes, and have the structure checked by an engineer before re-erecting it.

The buildings market has six categories: sheds and industrial buildings, warehouses and storage, workshops, farm sheds, livestock buildings, prefabricated kits and structures.

## 1. Reading a listing for an assembled building

A listing for an assembled building gives its shape, its condition and what the price includes. Compare this with your needs before any contact.

| Information | What you find there |
| --- | --- |
| Dimensions | Length, width, eaves height, number of portal frames, roof pitch (if given); the floor area is calculated |
| Structural system | Column / rafter portal frame, welded built-up section (PRS), lattice truss or other |
| Roof | Duo-pitch, mono-pitch, multi-span / sawtooth, curved or other |
| Roofing and cladding | Sandwich panels, profiled steel sheet, masonry (cladding), or none |
| Condition | New, used – excellent condition, used – good condition, to inspect or repair |
| What the price includes | Dismantling, transport, re-erection, foundations and accessories (anchor bolts, base plates, bolts) |
| Steel weight | Estimated from the listing data |

The listing page also shows an **indicative drawing** (elevation and plan with the portal frames), drawn only from the figures entered by the seller. It is not a structural study.

The asking price is for the whole building. A price that includes neither dismantling, transport nor re-erection is only the start of the cost: get quotes for this work before deciding.

## 2. Dismantled building: the inventory and the weight

A dismantled building is always listed with a **required inventory**, element by element. It is the most useful part of the listing: it says exactly what you are buying.

- Each line gives the element type, the section from the European catalogue, the quantity and the length.
- Element types: columns, rafters, trusses, purlins, cladding rails, bracing, gable posts, crane beams, other elements.
- The site calculates the unit weight and the total weight of each line. This is a **theoretical weight**, as for reuse.
- The price is per kilo (by default) or for the whole; the price per m² is calculated.

Check that the inventory is complete enough to re-erect a building: you need the columns, the rafters or trusses, the purlins, the bracing, and often the rails. A lot without bracing or without bolts means extra purchases.

## 3. Visit before buying

Nothing is paid on ReuSteel: you settle directly with the seller. For a building, a visit is essential.

1. **Measure**: length, width, eaves height, spacing of the portal frames. Compare with the listing.
2. **Check the sections** of the columns and rafters with a caliper.
3. **Look at the column bases and the connections**: corrosion, missing bolts, deformed base plates.
4. **Look for deformation**: a column out of plumb, a sagging rafter or purlin, vehicle impact.
5. **For a dismantled building**, tick off the inventory piece by piece and find the element markings.
6. **Ask for the drawings** and technical documents if they exist: they make re-erection and the study easier.

Write down what is agreed: elements included, condition, price, collection date, who dismantles.

## 4. Dismantling, transport and re-erection

A used building should be re-erected only after a qualified engineer has checked it. The page of every used building on ReuSteel says so.

The engineer checks the elements and connections and the corrosion, and redoes the calculation for the new site: wind, snow, soil and use are not the same as at the original site. The foundations must be designed for the new ground.

On ReuSteel:

- the **Engineering** page lets you request an inspection or a study; the platform sends the quote and assigns the job to an engineer verified on ReuSteel;
- the **Services** page lists companies for dismantling, erection, transport coordination, blasting and painting.

During dismantling, mark each element (painted number or tag) and keep the bolts by connection: re-erection will be faster and safer.

## 5. Selling your building on ReuSteel

Any ReuSteel account can list a building, except partner engineer accounts.

1. **Create the listing** in *My account → My listings*, Steel buildings market, and choose **assembled** or **dismantled**.
2. **Assembled**: enter the dimensions, structural system, roof, roofing, cladding, condition and what the price includes. **Dismantled**: fill in the inventory element by element.
3. **Add photos** (overall view, column bases, connections, defects) and the technical documents if you have them.
4. **Set the price**: for the whole building, or per kilo for a dismantled building.
5. **Reply to purchase requests** and offers in *Orders and offers received*: accept, decline or propose another price.

An honest listing sells better: show the defects in the photos and choose the real condition. The buyer will see them during the visit anyway.

## Checklist (buyer)

- [ ] The dimensions and structural system suit my project
- [ ] I know what the price includes (dismantling, transport, re-erection, foundations)
- [ ] I have quotes for what is not included
- [ ] For a dismantled building, the inventory is complete
- [ ] I have visited: measurements, sections, column bases, connections, deformation
- [ ] I have the drawings or technical documents, if they exist
- [ ] An engineer has checked the structure and redone the calculation for the new site
- [ ] The agreement is in writing: elements included, price, collection, who dismantles
